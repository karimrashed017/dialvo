# Dialvo: positioning and brand messaging

Written 19 November 2026, from a full read of trydialvo.com (home, pricing, the three motion pages, 14 plays and 23 platform pages) and the three plans.

## The honest starting point

Three facts decide everything below.

1. **The product is not built.** The site describes a full AI sales platform. Today there is a website, a waitlist and one working voice demo. Every claim on the site is a promise about the future.
2. **There is no proof.** No customers, no results, no case studies. A "revenue-generating" claim with no revenue to show is the weakest position a B2B company can take, however well it is written.
3. **Dialvo is the low-price entrant, not the premium one.** Comparable AI sales agents are sold through sales teams on annual contracts at several thousand dollars a month. $99 to $799 a month, self-serve, cancel anytime, is a fraction of that.

Good positioning has to work with these facts. Positioning that ignores them reads as hype and loses the sceptical buyer, who is the one with money.

## Where I disagree with your brief

**"Premium product."** I'd drop this word. Next to its real competitors Dialvo is five to fifteen times cheaper. That is the single most powerful fact about it, and "premium" hides it. The stronger frame is _serious capability at a price that needs no approval_: the kind of sales team only funded companies could afford, for less than a part-time hire. You can keep the brand feeling premium (design, tone, restraint) without claiming to be the expensive option.

**"Enterprise-ready."** Not true today, and enterprises would find out in one security questionnaire: no certifications, draft terms, no customers. Your buyers at $99 to $799 are agencies, founders and small sales teams. They want _fast, clear and trustworthy_, not enterprise. Drop it until it's real.

**"AI sales workforce."** It's a good idea but it isn't yours alone. "Digital workers" and "AI employees" are the standard language of this category, and "replace your sales team" messaging has drawn real backlash. Your own site already says something better and more believable: _agents open the conversations, your team closes them._ Position Dialvo as the team that makes your salespeople more valuable, not the thing that replaces them.

**"Revenue-generating."** Right direction, wrong claim. Dialvo doesn't generate revenue; the customer's offer and closers do. What Dialvo controls is the part before the sale: every lead answered, every follow-up sent, every account worked. Sell _no lead left unworked_, which is true and provable, and let the buyer do the revenue maths.

**The $99 plan fights the story.** A "sales workforce" for $99 is hard to believe. Either reposition Starter clearly as the _inbound desk_ (one job: answer and book every inbound lead), or accept that Pro is the real product and Starter is the way in. I recommend the first.

**"Free until your first booked meeting"** is your best offer and fully compatible with a serious brand. It says: we're paid when it works. Keep it and make it louder.

## 1. Positioning statement

> For agencies, founders and small sales teams who lose deals because nobody followed up fast enough, Dialvo is an AI sales team that answers every lead in seconds, works every account on every channel, and books the meetings, so your people spend their time closing. Unlike AI sales agents sold on annual contracts for thousands a month, Dialvo is self-serve, shows you every call and message, and costs nothing until it books your first meeting.

Short form: **The sales team that never lets a lead go cold.**

## 2. Ideal customer and highest-value segments

**Core profile:** a business with 2 to 50 people that sells something worth $1,000 or more per customer, already gets leads (forms, calls, referrals, ads), and has no dedicated person whose only job is to respond and follow up.

Ranked by value and ease of winning:

1. **Marketing, web and lead-generation agencies (Scale and Pro).** They feel the problem twice: their own pipeline, and clients who waste the leads the agency generates. Workspaces let them run Dialvo per client and resell it. Highest revenue per account, and each agency brings several end customers.
2. **High-ticket local services with paid lead flow (Pro and Starter):** home services, clinics and dental, legal intake, real estate teams, insurance and finance brokers. They pay for every lead and lose many to slow response. The value is obvious in one sentence.
3. **B2B software and services founders (Pro).** Founder-led sales, no SDR. They want outbound that doesn't embarrass them and inbound that doesn't wait for them.
4. **Small sales teams of 3 to 15 reps (Pro and Scale).** They want reps on live deals, not on research and chasing.

Avoid for now: enterprises (procurement and security you can't pass), and very low-ticket businesses (the maths doesn't work for them).

## 3. Three positioning strategies, ranked

**1. "No lead left unworked" (recommended).** Dialvo is the team that answers, follows up and books, on every channel, so nothing you paid for is wasted. Why it wins: it's true, it's specific, every buyer has felt the pain, and it needs no proof of revenue to be believed. It leads naturally with inbound, which is also the safest and fastest part of the product to build.

**2. "The sales team you couldn't afford, for the price of a tool."** Dialvo gives a small business the multi-channel sales operation that funded companies run. Why it's strong: it turns your price into the headline advantage. Why it's second: it invites comparison with expensive competitors before you have proof, and "cheap version of X" can cap how big the brand feels.

**3. "AI sales workforce" (your brief).** Dialvo is a workforce you hire instead of salespeople. Why it's third: it's the category's default claim, it promises replacement the product can't deliver, and it makes the buyer's own team feel threatened. Usable as a supporting idea ("your AI sales team"), not as the lead.

## 4. Hero headline and subheadline

Current: "AI sales agents that turn every conversation into pipeline." Accurate but abstract: it names a mechanism, not the pain or the win.

**Recommended:**

> **Every lead answered. Every follow-up sent. Every meeting booked.**
> Dialvo is your AI sales team. It calls new leads back within a minute, follows up by email, text and WhatsApp until they answer, and puts the meetings on your calendar. Your people do the closing.

Alternatives:

- **The sales team that never lets a lead go cold.**
- **You paid for those leads. Dialvo makes sure someone answers them.**

Main button: **Join the waitlist** (keep). Second button: **Talk to the agent** (keep; the live demo is the strongest proof you have, so point at it everywhere). Line under the buttons: **Free until it books your first meeting.**

## 5. Recommended homepage structure

1. **Hero:** the promise, the live demo button, the free-until-booked line.
2. **The cost of the problem:** what slow follow-up costs. No invented statistics; use the buyer's own maths.
3. **Hear it:** the live voice demo, moved up. It is the only proof on the site.
4. **What Dialvo does, in three jobs:** answer (inbound), reach (outbound), grow (expansion).
5. **Every channel, one team:** the six channel tabs.
6. **You stay in control:** see and hear everything, approve before send, handoff to a person.
7. **What it replaces:** the pile of tools and the hours, compared honestly.
8. **Built to be trusted:** the compliance rules, as a reason prospects will take the call.
9. **Pricing:** three plans, each tied to a kind of business.
10. **Questions,** then the final call to action.

What to cut or shrink on the home page: the 16-feature platform grid and the 14-play grid. They are for people already convinced; link to them, don't lead with them. The home page currently spends more words on features than on the problem.

## 6. Copy for each homepage section

**The cost of the problem**

> **The lead you didn't call back just hired someone else.**
> You paid to get that enquiry. If nobody answers for three hours, the person has moved on, and nobody follows up the ones who went quiet. It isn't a people problem. There are more leads, replies and follow-ups than any small team can keep up with.

**Hear it**

> **Don't take our word for it. Talk to it.**

**Three jobs**

> **Answer.** Every form, call and chat gets a reply in seconds, day or night. The agent asks your qualifying questions and books the ones worth your time.

**Every channel, one team**

> **One conversation, wherever they answer.**

**You stay in control**

> **Nothing happens out of sight.**

**What it replaces**

> **One team instead of five tools and a hire.**
> A dialer, an email sequencer, a chat widget, a lead database and someone to run them all. Dialvo is one system with one inbox, at a price you can decide on today.

**Built to be trusted**

> **Your name is on every call. We protect it.**

**Final call**

> **Stop paying for leads nobody answers.**
> Join the waitlist. It costs nothing until Dialvo books your first meeting.

## 7. Pricing page strategy

Fix first: the pricing headline on the home page still says "two simple plans". There are three.

Lead with the offer, not the price: **"Free until it books your first meeting."** Then name each plan after the job it does, so the buyer chooses by situation, not by feature count.

- **Starter, $99: the inbound desk.** _For a business that gets leads and loses them to slow replies._ One job done properly: every inbound lead answered, qualified and booked, with follow-up by email and text. "If you answer your own enquiries today, start here."
- **Pro, $299: the full sales team.** _For agencies and sales teams that want inbound, outbound and existing customers worked on every channel._ All channels, the human dialer for your reps, CRM sync. "This is the plan most teams should be on."
- **Scale, $799: for agencies and multi-brand teams.** _For running Dialvo across several clients or brands._ Five workspaces, higher limits, hands-on setup. "If you sell leads or sales as a service, this is how you deliver it."

Add one line of plain comparison under the cards, with no competitor names: _"A part-time sales assistant costs more than Scale. An agency retainer for outbound costs more than all three."_ Check any figure you use before publishing it.

Keep the limits visible and honest. Hidden limits are how AI tools lose trust.

## 8. Why it's an investment, not an expense

Do not print return-on-investment numbers; you have none. Give the buyer the sum and let them fill it in:

> **The maths is yours to do.** How many leads do you get a month? How many hear back within five minutes? What is one new customer worth? If answering faster wins you one extra customer a month, has Dialvo paid for itself?

For most businesses selling something worth $1,000 or more, one extra customer a month covers Pro several times over. That's an argument the buyer makes to themselves, which is the only kind they believe.

The second argument is cost of the alternative: a person to do this job costs a salary, takes months to hire and train, works eight hours and takes holidays. Dialvo starts this week and you can stop any month.

The third is risk: you pay nothing until it books a meeting.

## 9. Competitive differentiation

| Against                    | What they do                                              | Dialvo's honest edge                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sales dialers              | Help a rep make more calls                                | Dialvo does the answering, follow-up and booking itself; the dialer is one part, for your reps                                      |
| AI cold-calling tools      | AI voice phones strangers                                 | Dialvo's voice agents call people who asked. That's the difference between a welcome call and a nuisance, and it protects your name |
| Lead-generation tools      | Hand you a list                                           | A list isn't a meeting. Dialvo works the list until someone books                                                                   |
| CRM software               | Records what happened                                     | Dialvo makes things happen and writes them into your CRM                                                                            |
| Sales engagement platforms | Sequences a rep has to run                                | Dialvo writes, sends, answers replies and books, with you approving                                                                 |
| Single-channel AI agents   | One channel, no memory of the others                      | One team across voice, email, chat, social and messaging, with one history                                                          |
| Enterprise AI sales agents | Same idea, annual contracts, sales-led, thousands a month | Self-serve, monthly, visible in full, free until the first booked meeting                                                           |

The four differentiators worth building the brand on, in order:

1. **Free until the first booked meeting.** Nobody selling on annual contracts can match it.
2. **You see and hear everything.** The category's known weakness is black-box agents and inflated claims. Transparency is a position.
3. **One team across every channel,** at a self-serve price.
4. **It protects your reputation:** consent-based calling and honest AI disclosure, built in.

Do not claim: better AI, better voices or better data. You can't prove them and neither can anyone else.

## 10. The biggest messaging mistakes on the current site

1. **It leads with the product, not the pain.** The hero explains what agents do before saying what the buyer is losing.
2. **Too much, too soon.** 41 pages and 16 features for a product nobody has used reads as a catalogue. Depth is good for search; it shouldn't be the home page.
3. **No proof, and the one proof is buried.** The live voice demo should be the second thing anyone sees.
4. **The promise outruns the product.** The site reads as if everything works today. The FAQ admits it doesn't. A sharp buyer notices the gap. Say plainly, near the top: "Opening to teams in small groups, starting with inbound."
5. **"Pipeline" is jargon.** Agency owners and clinic owners say customers, enquiries and bookings.
6. **"Two simple plans"** while showing three.
7. **Starter is described by features,** not by the job it does.
8. **Compliance is written defensively.** It's a selling point: prospects pick up and stay on the line when a call is honest.
9. **Sample numbers in the dashboard** ("48 conversations, 4 meetings booked") are labelled as examples, but people skim. Make sure nobody can mistake them for results.
10. **Nothing says who it's for.** Name agencies and the industries you want on the home page.

## 11. The guiding brand message

> **Dialvo makes sure no lead goes unanswered and no opportunity goes unworked. Its agents open the conversations on every channel; your team closes them. You see everything it does, and you pay nothing until it books your first meeting.**

Voice: plain, confident, specific. Say what it does and what it costs. No "revolutionise", no "unlock", no promises of revenue. A brand feels serious when it doesn't need to shout.

## What matters more than any of this

Positioning multiplies proof; it can't replace it. The fastest way to make Dialvo feel like essential revenue infrastructure is three real customers with real booked meetings and their permission to say so. Build the inbound desk (speed to lead, follow-up, booking) first, get those three, and put their numbers where the sample dashboard is now. Every line above gets twice as strong the day you can do that.
